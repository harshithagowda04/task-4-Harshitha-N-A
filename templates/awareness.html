Phishing / Awareness

Attackers exploit attention and trust. Knowing the patterns helps break the chain.

THE CORE IDEA

Phishing is social engineering delivered through a message.

An attacker impersonates a person or organization to trick someone into sharing information, sending money, or opening a harmful link or file.

Email phishing

Bulk email impersonation, fake invoices, account warnings and delivery notices designed to trigger a quick click.

“Your account will close today. Verify now.”

SMS phishing · Smishing

Text messages use delivery, toll, payment or security alerts to push a short link.

“Unpaid toll fee. Settle it here.”

Voice phishing · Vishing

A caller may pretend to be a bank, support desk or authority and ask for codes or payment.

“Read me the one-time code to stop fraud.”

Spear phishing

Targeted messages use personal, role or company details to feel credible and relevant.

“Please review this confidential project file.”

Fake login pages

A link leads to a copied sign-in page that captures credentials, MFA codes or session details.

Check the full domain before entering credentials.

Malicious attachments

Unexpected files may exploit software or persuade you to enable unsafe macros or scripts.

Confirm unexpected files with the sender first.
RECOGNIZE THE SIGNALS

Pause when a message…

  • Creates urgency, fear or secrecy
  • Requests passwords, codes, payment or gift cards
  • Uses a mismatched, shortened or oddly spelled domain
  • Includes an unexpected attachment or prize
  • Greets you generically or has an unusual sender address
RESPOND SAFELY

Break the attack chain

  1. Don’t click, reply, or open the attachment.
  2. Visit the official site through a bookmark or known address.
  3. Contact the person using a known phone number or channel.
  4. Report it using your organization’s phishing report process.
  5. If you shared information, tell IT/security and change credentials promptly.
